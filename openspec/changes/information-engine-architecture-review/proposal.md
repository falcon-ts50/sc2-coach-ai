# Change: Information Engine Architecture Review

## Status

Proposed — REVIEW only. Production implementation is not authorized by this change packet.

## Problem

The Information Engine is being implemented while task instructions have been transferred through multiple Telegram messages. The agent began work before receiving the complete instruction set. This creates a material risk that implementation scope, contracts and architectural placement differ from the intended design.

A production support bundle generated by application version `0.7.0`, commit `b30d8ce4d450`, confirms a product-level gap: the report exposes match summary, turning points and combat episodes, but no explicit scouting or information-acquisition analysis. It also remains centred on combat outcome and losses rather than explaining how players prepared between engagements.

The intended capability is broader than combat efficiency. SC2 Coach must eventually explain competing strategic choices across the intervals between engagements, including questions such as:

- who invested in workers and future economy;
- who produced immediate fighting units;
- who added production capacity;
- who teched or upgraded;
- who expanded;
- who accumulated or spent resources;
- who scouted, scanned or otherwise acquired information;
- what each player could reasonably know before choosing a strategy;
- whether teams synchronized preparation for the next engagement.

## Desired outcome

Produce a reviewable, evidence-backed architecture description for an Information Engine that models both information state and strategic preparation over time.

The review must establish:

- the exact responsibility of the Information Engine;
- its inputs, outputs and invariants;
- how scouting observations and player knowledge are represented without omniscient leakage;
- how intervals between engagements are identified and analysed;
- how player and team investments are classified and compared;
- its relationship to Context, Decision, Turning Point, Knowledge, Combat and narrative components;
- which module owns it;
- deterministic versus heuristic behaviour;
- confidence and evidence semantics;
- failure and partial-data behaviour;
- compatibility and migration impact;
- test strategy and acceptance scenarios;
- discrepancies between intended architecture and current code.

## Scope

- Inspect current Information Engine implementation and tests.
- Identify the implementation branch or PR and its base.
- Inspect support-bundle/report behaviour relevant to scouting and between-fight preparation.
- Map current code to existing architecture and ADRs.
- Define whether the canonical analysis unit is an event, observation, state snapshot, interval, engagement boundary or a combination of these.
- Define player-perspective information state separately from omniscient replay facts.
- Define a preparation-allocation model covering at least economy, immediate army, technology/upgrades, production capacity, expansion, defence and information acquisition.
- Define comparison semantics for individual players and teams, including asynchronous preparation.
- Complete `design.md` with concrete findings and proposed contracts.
- Create or update `specs/information-engine/spec.md` with normative requirements and scenarios.
- Update `tasks.md` with evidence and any required follow-up implementation tasks.
- Report contradictions, hidden coupling, duplicated responsibilities and premature abstractions.

## Non-goals

- Do not modify production code.
- Do not refactor existing engines.
- Do not implement scouting detection in this REVIEW gate.
- Do not reduce the Information Engine to combat winner or combat-efficiency scoring.
- Do not assume that replay omniscience equals player knowledge.
- Do not infer player intent as fact.
- Do not merge or retarget existing PRs.
- Do not redefine accepted ADRs without proposing a separate ADR change.
- Do not claim the design is validated when relevant code, tests or replay evidence were unavailable.

## Completion condition

The REVIEW phase is complete when the design and capability specification are sufficiently precise that a separate APPLY task can implement:

1. player-perspective information acquisition and knowledge state;
2. between-engagement strategic preparation comparison;
3. downstream explanations connecting observed information, preparation choices and later engagements;

without relying on Telegram history or unstated assumptions.
